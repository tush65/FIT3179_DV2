Why Do People Use The Internet

Now that we have seen how people use it in various states and territories, we will see the reasons for people accessing the internet. This radar chart shows a clear divide, with 4 out of 6 services having a high use rate of around 80%.

Services such as purchasing goods, social media, entertainment and banking show very high rates of use online. Digital platforms act as the primary way to watch entertainment and social media, streaming, and making connections.

On the other hand, health services and education show less digital integration, showing that medical consultations and formal learning often retain traditional in-person components.

Technology Use Across Demographics

Let us now compare the different uses for technology across Age Group, to figure out who is the most tech savvy, and who is lagging behind.

The Dark Side of Digital Banking

Even though the closing of ATMS mean that we use banking more digitally, a consequence of this is that it has resulted in an increase in online scams.

Last year, 2025, reported around $2.18 billion lost to online scams, and the most common types are investment and payment redirection, which directly correlate to digital banking.

In fact, the amount lost to other methods such as remote acccess and romance scams has reduced since 2024, and shows that scammers are doubling down on banking details, and are obtaining it more and more through phishing, which is also a problem caused by increased online shopping.

What do Australians Do On Social Media?

Overall, Australians tend to use social media for reading private messages and seeing posts from people they follow.

This is significantly higher than viewing posts that are suggested by the platform itself, showing that broadly speaking, Social Media is primarily used to maintain connections with people, which is actually the original reason for having Social Media since its inception.

We can see that overall, the internet usage rate even amongst the most disadvantaged demographic groups maintains a strong baseline of at least 65%, demonstrating idespread digital access across Australia.

Household income rates creates the biggest digital divide in Australia, with around 69% access rate for the lowest income bracket, and a near 100% usage rate for the highest earners. While this makes sense, some factors which are expected to play a big role, such as remoteness, show only a minor difference of around 7% in the usage rate.
