showing how citizens engage in everyday activities
such as banking, shopping, education, healthcare and more.

This dashboard will explore how technology
is actively being used in modern Australian society,
while also showing some challenges,
such as current gaps in
usage across demographics.

Why Do People Use The Internet?

Now that we have seen how people use it in various states and territories, we will see the reasons for people accessing the internet. This radar chart shows a clear divide, with 4 out of 6 services having a high use rate of around 80%.

Services such as purchasing goods, social media, entertainment and banking show very high rates of use online. Digital platforms act as the primary way to watch entertainment and social media, streaming, and making connections.

On the other hand, health services and education show less digital integration, showing that medical consultations and formal learning often retain traditional in-person components.

Figure 3: Radar Chart Showing Percentage of Australians
Using Different Technology Services

Technology Use Across Demographics

Let us now compare the different uses for technology across Age Group, to figure out who is the most tech savvy, and who is lagging behind.

Digital engagement peaks strongly among younder demographics. Particularly, those aged 18-34 dominate in areas such as banking and entertainment where usage exceeds 85%.

Conversely, traditional sectors like formal education show a sharp decline with age, as people being to do less formal education after their 20s, while health services show consistently low usage levels across all demographics, highlighting key areas for future digital growth.

What do Australians shop for?

Within shopping, we will now explore what people are buying in particular, split across many categories.

This follows the trends that were observed in the previous visualisation, with areas such as entertainment, cloting and travel showing relatively high use cases at around 50%.

However, areas such as Health, Cars, Food and Toys show quite low usage rates, all below 30%. This could be due to the fact that people prefer to see the physical items, such as Cars and Food before commiting to it, and people prefer to also see health professionals in person.

Who Uses Telehealth?

We can see that there is an interesting phenomenon that happens here.

Roughly 1 in 4 people have used telehealth, and around 9 in 10 of them say they would use it again. This points to strong satisfaction among those who have tried it, and it suggests the service delivers what people expect.

The Dark Side of Digital Banking

Even though the closing of ATMS mean that we use banking more digitally, a consequence of this is that it has resulted in an increase in online scams.

Last year, 2025, reported around $2.18 billion lost to online scams, and the most common types are investment and payment redirection, which directly correlate to digital banking.

In fact, the amount lost to other methods such as remote acccess and romance scams has reduced since 2024, and shows that scammers are doubling down on banking details, and are obtaining it more and more through phishing, which is also a problem caused by increased online shopping.

What do Australians Do On Social Media?

Overall, Australians tend to use social media for reading private messages and seeing posts from people they follow.

This is significantly higher than viewing posts that are suggested by the platform itself, showing that broadly speaking, Social Media is primarily used to maintain connections with people, which is actually the original reason for having Social Media since its inception.

Figure 10: Faceted Donut Chart of what Australians Do on Social Media

Why do Australians use Social Media?
(A Further Breakdown)

Social Media is one of the main reasons why people use the internet...
but what features do they actually use?

We can see that across mainly Snapchat and Facebook, people primarily use it in order to talk to others and catch up on mainly their close social circle, rather than on exploring new content and discovering new things. This is reinforced by the treemap's emphasis on features like Chat, Messenger, Map and Groups over public trend feeds.

Instagram balances discovery with private sharing. Users explore trends, reels and creators via Explore and Reels and frequently send those discoveries to their close circle.

TikTok truly stands out by being an entertainment network rather than a traditional social network. The majority of content that users consume comes from the For You page and the search bar.

Factors Influencing Access to Technology

Now that we have seen how age affects internet usage rate, and focused on particular activities, let us round off by examining what gaps still remain in technology acccess, and what factors influence that.

We can see that overall, the internet usage rate even amongst the most disadvantaged demographic groups maintains a strong baseline of at least 65%, demonstrating idespread digital access across Australia.

Household income rates creates the biggest digital divide in Australia, with around 69% access rate for the lowest income bracket, and a near 100% usage rate for the highest earners. While this makes sense, some factors which are expected to play a big role, such as remoteness, show only a minor difference of around 7% in the usage rate.

Figure 12: Connected Dot Plot showing Gaps in Internet Usage

Conclusion and Data Sources

Taken together, these charts show a digital Australia that is widely connected but unevenly experienced. Internet access is high by global standards, yet gaps exist in how people use it: usage falls with age, income levels and employment status.

Formal education and health services lag well behind other areas. Online shopping is highest for entertainment and clothing, and weakest for large purchases such as cars and gambling. Telehealth shows that the main barrier to adoption is a willingness to try it, not the service itself, as indicated by high satisfaction.

Along with this, ATM closures cluster in urban areas whilst regional locations keep or gain machines, which can cause increased scam risks such as payment redirection and phishing.
